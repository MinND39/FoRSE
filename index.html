Abstract

Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) reliably recognize objects and scenes. Yet they often suffer from temporal hallucinations: they assert temporal information, such as the order or direction of events, that is not supported by the video. Existing training-free approaches either amplify per-frame salient evidence or contrast predictions with those from temporally corrupted videos. They implicitly assume that directing the model to the right regions recovers the correct temporal decision. However, attending to the right regions in a single frame does not automatically transfer to temporal understanding. Our diagnostics show that the final decoding stage has limited ability to retain inter-frame temporal evidence. Analyzing this failure further, we find that the representations at this stage lack most of the temporal evidence. The model also does not read out what little evidence is present when producing the answer.

We therefore propose FoRSE, a training-free scheme that strengthens the foreground evidence and its temporal change in the visual tokens before they reach the language model. This makes the temporal evidence easier for the model to use when decoding the answer. Without updating any model parameters, FoRSE reinforces persistent foreground regions and injects foreground-gated inter-frame feature residuals into the visual representations. Across three backbones (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, and LLaVA-Video), FoRSE improves average accuracy over the unmodified models in all six backbone-benchmark combinations on VideoHallucer and EventHallusion, with gains of up to 4.38 and 4.59 percentage points, respectively.

Diagnosing Temporal Hallucination

Three observations

  • F1. Foreground attention. When LLaVA-OV-7B answers correctly, it places more attention on the salient foreground and keeps it across more frames (Table 1).
  • F2. No explicit change. Visual tokens describe each frame on its own: one group of tokens describes “an upright cup”, a later group “a cup on its side”, but no token says “the cup fell”.
  • F3. Weak use of motion. On time-reversed TempCompass clips, the decision-state probe rarely flips, and the model's own horizontal direction accuracy is at chance (Table 2).

Method

No trainingOne forward passMasks once per videoNo tracking or optical flow

Persistence-guided spatial enhancement

Each visual token is scaled by the fraction of it covered by the foreground at step , and by how often the foreground occupies its position over the clip, so that a frame whose mask misses the subject still inherits it from the others.

Foreground-gated temporal residual

The spatial term does not depend on the order of the steps. We add the change between adjacent steps to the tokens, norm-matched so that one coefficient controls its strength, and gated by the foreground.

FoRSE needs no training, no optical flow and no tracking. It runs a single forward pass of the Video-LLM, and the masks are computed once per video. For it reduces exactly to the original model.

Analysis

What makes it work

Each spatial term alone mostly helps motion recognition, and the temporal residual alone helps camera motion most; the full method is best on all six metrics. Replacing the residual with a random vector of the same norm, or with one computed from shuffled frames, removes most of the gain.

Inference cost

FoRSE runs a single forward pass and computes the masks once per video. It costs 1.30× the base latency on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 2.88× on LLaVA-OV-7B, against 2.32× and 5.73× for SEASON.
